GLORB // EMPATHY MISSION

GLORB // EMPATHY MISSION is an interactive explicit-teaching lesson. Students help Glorb notice social and emotional clues, make a careful guess about what another person might be experiencing, check rather than assume, and choose a response that fits what the person may need.

WHAT ARE STUDENTS LEARNING?

The lesson uses one repeated process:

NOTICE → CAREFUL GUESS → CHECK → RESPOND

NOTICE more than one clue. CAREFUL GUESS using words such as might. CHECK because the first interpretation may be wrong. RESPOND with words or actions that fit what the person may need.

WHY USE A CAREFUL GUESS?

A facial expression, body position or behaviour can give useful information, but it does not prove how somebody feels. The mission therefore asks students to combine clues from the person’s face, body, words and situation before making a tentative guess.

The checking step is important because empathy is not the same as decoding a face. Students are encouraged to stay open to being wrong and communicate with the other person.

AUSTRALIAN CURRICULUM CONNECTION

The mission aligns most directly with the Australian Curriculum Version 9.0 Personal and Social capability, especially Social Awareness: Empathy, with supporting links to Relational Awareness, Communication and Decision-making [1].

ACARA describes the capability as supporting students to understand themselves and others and navigate relationships.

WHY TEACH IT EXPLICITLY?

Some social expectations are unwritten and can be difficult to infer. The mission makes the reasoning process visible by breaking it into small, named steps and giving students guided practice.

Research on school-based social and emotional learning supports structured, sequenced, active, focused and explicit teaching approaches [2].

WHY DOES THE FEEDBACK EXPLAIN THE CLUES?

The activities are designed as formative practice rather than a score of social competence. Feedback names the exact clues that support or weaken a choice, explains the reasoning, and lets the student try again.

This is consistent with explicit teaching and feedback approaches in the Victorian High Impact Teaching Strategies [3].

WHY IS THE INFORMATION BROKEN INTO SMALL STEPS?

Short instructions, repeated layouts, one main task at a time and relevant images reduce unnecessary processing demands. The aim is to simplify the hidden social rule, not make the learner feel younger.

The design is consistent with research on multimedia learning and segmentation, where information is divided into meaningful parts so attention can remain on the learning itself [4].

WHY IS THE STUDENT THE EARTH HELPER?

Glorb is positioned as the learner. The student helps an alien understand confusing Earth behaviour instead of being positioned as the person whose social skills need fixing.

This role reversal supports a low-pressure, age-respectful learning experience and allows foundational social concepts to be taught without using an infantile visual or conversational register.

HOW TO SUPPORT A STUDENT

  • Let the student attempt each item before prompting.
  • Ask, “Which clues helped you decide?”
  • Ask, “Could those clues mean something else?”
  • Ask, “What could you check with the person?”
  • Use the same NOTICE → CAREFUL GUESS → CHECK → RESPOND sequence in later classroom discussion or role-play.
Transfer matters: completing a digital scenario does not prove that the skill will automatically be used in a real interaction. Discussion, observation and practice in natural settings remain important.

IMPORTANT LIMITS

The Empathy Mission is an educational practice activity. It is not a clinical, medical or psychological assessment.

It does not measure empathy as a trait and it does not determine what another person is actually feeling. People can express the same emotion in different ways, and the same visible clue can have more than one explanation.

The research base supports the teaching and design rationale used in GLORB. It does not establish that this individual GLORB lesson has been independently proven to improve outcomes.
