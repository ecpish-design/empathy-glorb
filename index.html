About the Empathy Mission

What is it?

GLORB // Empathy Mission is an explicit-teaching interactive lesson. Students help Glorb notice clues, make a careful guess, check what someone may need and choose a kind response.

What it is for

It makes empathy reasoning more visible by teaching one simple sequence: NOTICE → CAREFUL GUESS → CHECK → RESPOND.

Learning focus

Students practise using more than one clue, staying open to being wrong, asking instead of assuming and choosing a response that fits the situation.

Teaching aid, not a real-life test

Completion shows supported performance inside this lesson. It does not prove that a student can use the skill independently in every real-life interaction.

Learning sequence

NOTICE → CAREFUL GUESS → CHECK → RESPOND

Notice

Look for more than one clue from the person’s face, body, words and situation.

Careful guess

Think about what the person might be feeling. A clue can help, but it is not proof.

Check

Ask what the person wants or needs instead of deciding for them.

Respond

Choose kind words or help that fit. Respect the person’s answer.

Students practise

Combining clues, using tentative language, checking interpretations, and choosing between help, listening, company or space.

Explicit teaching

The mission breaks an unwritten social skill into named steps, then gives guided practice and feedback.

Careful interpretation

The lesson does not teach that a face or behaviour reveals a feeling for certain. Students use several clues and then check.

Segmentation

One main task is shown at a time so students can focus on the reasoning instead of holding the whole lesson in mind.

Formative feedback

Wrong answers explain why the choice fits less well and allow another attempt. Correct answers also explain the clues.

Age-respectful design

Language and task load can be simplified without making the visual style or social situations feel younger than the learner.

Important

This is an educational practice activity. It is not a clinical, medical or psychological assessment.

Use with in-person teaching

Pair the mission with discussion, adult modelling, role-play and real examples outside the screen.

Clues are contextual

The same clue can have more than one meaning. Encourage students to stay curious and check rather than assume.

Support is individual

Different people may want help, listening, company or space. The lesson gives examples, not one fixed rule for everyone.

How to support a student

Let the student try first. Then ask: Which clues helped you decide? Could those clues mean something else? What could you ask the person?

Interpret completion carefully

The research base supports the teaching and design rationale used here. It does not show that completing this lesson proves real-world empathy or that GLORB itself has been independently proven effective.
